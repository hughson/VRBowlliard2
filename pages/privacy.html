Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

The Last Rack is a virtual reality game for Meta Quest, made by Deucejuice Studios. This policy explains what the game collects, why, who else handles it, how long it's kept, and how to have it deleted.

Questions or requests: vrbowlliards2@proton.me. You can also use this address to report a security problem.

What the game collects, and why

Your Meta account details. Your Meta user ID, username and profile details, from Meta. We use them to sign you in, keep your progress, and show your name to other players.

Your progress. Your ice balance (the game's play money) and what it's spent on, game results, scores, ratings, stats, poker results, tournament entries and purchases of game content. We use these to run the game. Ice can't be exchanged for real money.

Leaderboards. Your name and figures such as scores and monthly takings appear on public leaderboards, in the game and on this website.

What other players see in a room. Your name, avatar, head and hand movements and what you do in the game are sent live to other players in your room. Your Meta user ID is also shared with the game on their headsets, so the games can tell players apart.

Public rooms on the website. Open rooms, with their names, player counts and live pictures of the tables, may be shown on the game's website. Voice is never included.

Live voice chat. If your mic is on, your voice is sent live to other players in your room. It isn't stored on the way, except as described below.

Tournament messages. Messages you send to agree a match time are sent to the other player.

Reports. If you report someone, or someone reports you, we keep both names and Meta user IDs, the reason given, the room, the time, and the game version. A person reads these to deal with broken rules. Someone you report isn't told who reported them.

Bans. If you're banned for how you've behaved, we keep the length, the reason and how many times it's happened.

Security alerts. We flag unusual activity, such as very large ice gains, to protect the game's economy. These can include player names and IDs.

Darts calibration (optional). After you calibrate your darts throw, the game can share the results with a random code, your headset model and its graphics chip, with no name or account. You can turn this off on the Credits page.

On your headset only. Pictures and songs you add yourself, and most of your settings, stay on your headset. They aren't sent to us or to other players.

We don't sell your data, we don't show ads, and we don't use your data to work out who you are in real life.

Voice recording for reports

This only applies if we've turned this feature on, and only if you agree. The game asks you once.

If you agree:

  • While you play, your headset keeps the last 2 minutes of voice in its memory: yours, and that of other players in the room who've also agreed. It keeps being overwritten, it's never saved to your headset's storage, and closing the game erases it.
  • It's only sent if someone reports a player. Then those 2 minutes are sent to us with the report, with each person's voice kept separately.
  • Why: so that a report of abuse can be checked, including who said what first.
  • Who hears it: the game's developer, to decide what to do about the report. We don't use it for anything else, we don't use it to identify people by their voice, and we don't use it to train AI.
  • How long: it's deleted automatically after 30 days. We may keep it longer only if the law requires us to, for example if it's evidence of a serious crime.

This means that if you agree, your voice can be sent to us because of a report made by somebody else.

If you say no: your mic stays off and your headset keeps no voice. You can still hear other players and play the whole game.

Why voice chat depends on this: reports of abuse in voice chat can't be checked otherwise, and if some players could talk without being recorded, the people most likely to abuse others could simply choose that. The rest of the game doesn't depend on it.

Changing your mind: in a room, open the menu's Online page and press Voice recording. Saying no stops the recording straight away and wipes what's in your headset's memory. To have clips already sent to us deleted, email the address above.

Who gets recorded: players on older versions of the game, who were never asked, aren't recorded.

Who else handles your data

  • Meta runs the platform and handles your account and purchases.
  • Google (Firebase) stores game data, reports and voice clips, on servers in the United States.
  • Exit Games (Photon) carries multiplayer data, live voice and tournament messages between players.
  • Discord, only if we've set up alerts: alerts about reports and unusual activity, with player names but no voice, may go to a private developer channel.

Your data may be handled outside your own country. We may also share data if the law requires it, or to report a serious threat or harm to a child to the authorities.

Your choices and rights

You can ask us to show you, correct or delete your data by emailing vrbowlliards2@proton.me from the address on your Meta account, or by telling us your Meta username. Some places give you further rights by law, and you can complain to your local privacy regulator.

We may need to keep some records for a while if the law requires it, or to keep a ban in force. We'll tell you if that applies.

Children

The Last Rack is for people aged 13 and over. It isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly keep their data. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game, please email us, and we'll delete what we hold about them, including any voice.

Security

Data is sent over encrypted connections and kept on access-controlled servers. Only the developer can reach reports and voice clips. No system is perfectly secure: if a breach affects you, we'll tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and doesn't track visitors. The leaderboards and the count of players online are read by your browser straight from the game's database at Google (Firebase), and the fonts come from Google Fonts, so your browser contacts Google to fetch them. Leaderboard figures are kept in your browser for a few minutes so the page doesn't have to fetch them again; closing the tab clears them.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date above. If a change affects how voice is used, for example a new purpose, we'll ask you again before it applies to you.
