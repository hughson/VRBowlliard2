About The Last Rack

A Deucejuice Studios game for Meta Quest.

The Last Rack is a pool hall that stays open late. There are pool tables down the room, dartboards on the wall, a poker table in the corner and a jukebox full of blues. Come in on your own, or with a room full of people.

Pool

Eight ball, nine ball and cowboy pool against another player, or free play whenever you just want to hit balls. Shots are worked out with a physically-based model of the cue strike rather than a simple push: where the tip lands gives you english, draw and follow, lifting the butt gives you jumps and massé, and sidespin takes the cue ball off the aim line the way a real tip does. Cushions, throw and ball-to-ball collisions are modelled too.

Bowlliards

Bowling, played on a pool table. Ten frames, two visits a frame, ten object balls on the cloth. Run all ten in one visit and you've struck; clear them in two and you've spared. The scoring is bowling's, with strikes and spares carrying into the next frame, but every point comes off a cue ball. Play it solo, head to head, or as a pair in co-op.

Bowl a perfect 300. That's all we're saying.

Darts

Three boards, four games: 501, 301, Cricket and Around the World. Throw on your own or challenge someone to a match.

Poker

Texas Hold'em at a six-seat table, played with your hands instead of buttons: throw your cards in to fold, tap the felt to check, push your chips forward to bet. Sit down with friends, and the house regulars will take any empty seats. Poker is played for ice, the pub's play money, which can't be exchanged for real money.

Blues

The jukebox plays original blues written for the pub, with a few seasonal records when the calendar calls for them.

Play together

Public rooms are open to anyone and show up in the room list. Private rooms are the same pub, unlisted: share the code with whoever you want to let in. Voice chat carries between players in the same room. The leaderboards on the pub wall are on this site too, out to the top 100.

Controllers or a real cue

Play with controllers, or attach one to a real cue for a stroke that matches the one you'd make at a table. Mixed reality puts the table in your own room.
